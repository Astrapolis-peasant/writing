# Male Disposability

The claim that societies systematically allocate physical risk, hazardous labor,
and expendability to men, and that this asymmetry goes largely unexamined because
it is coded as male privilege rather than male cost.

This is the checkable layer of [[The Manipulated Man (Vilar)]] — the part that
makes empirical claims rather than psychological assertions, and the part most
worth testing against data the book itself never supplies.

## Vilar's catalogue

Enumerated in the 2008 author's introduction as the original book's core
disadvantages ([[The Manipulated Man (Vilar)]]):

| Claim | Status as stated |
|---|---|
| Men are conscripted; women are not | Verifiable, jurisdiction-dependent |
| Men are sent to fight wars; women are not | Verifiable, has shifted since 1971 |
| Men retire later despite shorter life expectancy | Verifiable, jurisdiction-dependent |
| Men have no reproductive veto — no pill, no abortion | Structurally true as stated |
| Men support women; women rarely support men | Empirical, contested, has shifted |
| Men work all their lives; women temporarily or not | Empirical, substantially changed |
| Men work more yet are on average poorer | Empirical, needs definition of "poorer" |
| Men "borrow" children; women keep them after separation | Empirical, has shifted |

Vilar concedes movement on only one point — women's workforce participation — and
argues the rest consolidated rather than eased. She notes that where women gained
access to military careers, they gained it without conscription, and where
retirement ages equalized, they equalized upward for women rather than downward
for men.

## Why it is separable from the rest of the book

The catalogue stands or falls on records: conscription statutes, combat deaths,
pension ages, custody outcomes, workplace fatality rates. None of it depends on
Vilar's assertions about female psychology — that women lack feelings, that their
intelligence atrophies — which are unsupported and carry the book's reputational
weight. The two layers are logically independent but were published bound together,
which is the practical reason the checkable layer went unexamined for decades.

> [!warning] Unsourced
> Vilar supplies no data for any item above. Treat the table as a list of
> hypotheses to check, not findings. The status column is this wiki's assessment of
> what *kind* of claim each is, not a verdict on truth.

## Where the argument went next

The same structural claims — male expendability, one-way protective obligation,
the reading of male dominance as burden rather than privilege — resurfaced in the
1990s men's-rights literature with actual statistics attached, most prominently in
Warren Farrell's *The Myth of Male Power* (1993).
