# Esther Vilar

Argentine-born writer, trained in medicine and sociology, author of the 1971
polemic [[The Manipulated Man (Vilar)]].

> [!warning] Thin page
> Everything below comes from a single source — the book's own jacket copy and
> author's introduction. Vilar is describing herself, which makes this the weakest
> possible provenance for biographical claims. Needs a second, independent source.

## From the source

Born in Buenos Aires; studied medicine and sociology before writing. Followed
[[The Manipulated Man (Vilar)]] with further work on relations between the sexes —
the novels *The Mosquito*, *The Mathematics of Nina Gluckstein*, and *The Seven
Fires of Mademoiselle*, and plays including *The American Popess*, *Speer*, and
*Jealousy*. Also *The Polygamous Sex*.

## On the book's reception

In the 2008 introduction she reports the aftermath in her own words: isolation,
consequences for her subsequent writing and private life, and violent threats that
"have not ceased to this date." She describes being cast publicly as a
"misogynist," "reactionary," and "fascist" for defending men — and notes that most
of the handful of people who defended her were women.

Her stated position is not anti-feminist in aim but in method: she says she wanted
to change the destiny of her sex, and thought the women's movement was attempting
it dishonestly ([[Vilar contra Second-Wave Feminism]]).
