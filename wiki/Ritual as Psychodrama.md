# Ritual as Psychodrama

Ceremony performed by someone who knows it is theatre, for effects that occur in
the performer rather than the cosmos — the mechanism [[LaVeyan Satanism]] proposes
in place of belief.

The formulation in [[The Satanic Bible (LaVey)]] is unusually candid about it: the
ritual chamber is an "intellectual decompression chamber," and its trappings —
bells, candles, chants, Enochian recitation — exist to suspend analysis long
enough for emotion to build. "The Satanist KNOWS he is practicing a form of
contrived ignorance in order to expand his will, whereas another religionist
doesn't."

## The claim underneath

That accepting something intellectually and accepting it emotionally are separate
capacities, and that the second has no modern provision. Psychiatry addressed the
intellect and left the appetite for ceremony unmet. Ritual is therefore retained
as technology, stripped of the metaphysics that once justified it.

Its stated function is discharge. Anger, lust, and grief are gathered, spent inside
a bounded time and place, and thereby prevented from leaking into ordinary life —
"the purpose of the ritual is to FREE the magician from thoughts that would consume
him." Anxiety after the fact is treated as failure, since it means the emotion was
never actually released.

## The three ceremonies

Sex, compassion, and destruction — mapped to desire, sentiment, and anger. Each
prescribes a different bodily terminus: orgasm, weeping, or hatred sustained to
exhaustion. The consistency is telling. The ritual is judged by whether the body
completed something, not by whether the world changed.

## Where the honesty runs out

The frame is only half-held. Having explained ritual as psychodrama, the book also
asserts that curses cause real harm at a distance, that spells land best when the
target is asleep, and that a woman's menstrual cycle should be timed against.
[[Unfalsifiable Inversion]] describes the escape hatch: skeptics are said to be
*more* vulnerable, and any misfortune afterward counts as confirmation.

So two theories sit in one book — ritual as self-administered catharsis, which is
plausible and checkable, and ritual as remote causation, which is neither. The
first survives detachment from the second.

## Transferable residue

The durable observation is that abandoning a belief does not abolish the appetite
the belief was serving, and that ceremony can be knowingly used for effects that
are entirely psychological — a claim about human need that does not require any of
the surrounding metaphysics, and one that would be recognizable to anyone who has
kept a ritual after losing the faith that came with it.
