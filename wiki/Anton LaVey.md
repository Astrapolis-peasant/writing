# Anton LaVey

American occultist (1930–1997) who founded the [[Church of Satan]] in 1966 and
wrote [[The Satanic Bible (LaVey)]], giving [[LaVeyan Satanism]] its name and
canonical form.

## The account the book gives

Born in Chicago, raised in California. Grandmother from Eastern Europe supplied the
Transylvanian folklore; he read *Dracula*, *Frankenstein*, and *Weird Tales* young.
Dropped out of high school, joined the Clyde Beatty Circus as a cage boy, worked
with big cats, then played calliope and organ. Moved to a carnival, assisted a
magician, learned hypnosis. Studied criminology at San Francisco City College and
worked as a police photographer for three years.

The founding anecdote is told four times in this edition, nearly verbatim: playing
organ for burlesque on Saturday night and for tent revivalists on Sunday morning,
he watched the same men in both audiences and concluded "the Christian Church
thrives on hypocrisy, and that man's carnal nature will out."

On Walpurgisnacht 1966 he shaved his head, put on a clerical collar, and declared
the Church of Satan, renumbering the year as I *Anno Satanas*. Died 29 October
1997 of heart disease — "no deathbed repentance," per the 2005 introduction.

## On the biography's reliability

> [!warning] Self-reported and contested
> Nearly all of the above comes from LaVey or his own organization. Peter Gilmore's
> 2005 introduction concedes that "detractors of LaVey with rather obvious agendas
> have disputed the authenticity of some of the events chronicled" in the two
> biographies, and answers only that verifiable incidents "far outweigh the items
> in dispute" — while granting that LaVey "was a skilled showman, a talent he never
> denied." The claimed affair with Marilyn Monroe, the circus and police careers,
> and the celebrity memberships are exactly the sort of material a showman would
> embellish. Recorded here as claims, not facts; a hostile or independent source
> would settle it.

The self-mythology is not incidental to the philosophy. A man who taught that
"lesser magic" is the deliberate management of how one appears — sex, sentiment, or
wonder, deployed to command attention — was practicing his own doctrine when
constructing his biography.
