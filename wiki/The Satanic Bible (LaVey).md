# The Satanic Bible (LaVey)

The 1969 founding text of [[LaVeyan Satanism]], written by [[Anton LaVey]] for the
[[Church of Satan]] — an **atheistic** philosophy of indulgence, self-interest, and
ritual psychodrama in which Satan is a symbol, not a being.

The single most common misreading is that it describes devil worship. It does the
opposite: it rejects worship as such. "The worship of any deity or deities — under
any guise whatsoever — is repulsive to the Black Magician." Satan names a stance,
not an entity: "the spirit of revolt that leads to freedom."

## Structure

Four books on the classical elements, prefaced by the Nine Satanic Statements:

| Book | Element | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Satan | Fire | "The Infernal Diatribe" — aphoristic invective in King James cadence |
| Lucifer | Air | The philosophy proper; twelve essays, the substance of the book |
| Belial | Earth | Theory and mechanics of magic; ritual instructions |
| Leviathan | Water | Invocations, the Infernal Names, nineteen Enochian Keys |

The Nine Statements compress the whole: indulgence over abstinence, vital
existence over spiritual pipe dreams, undefiled wisdom over hypocritical
self-deceit, kindness to the deserving over love wasted on ingrates, vengeance
over turning the other cheek, responsibility to the responsible, man as just
another animal, all sins as gratification, and — the joke that is also the
argument — "Satan has been the best friend the church has ever had, as he has kept
it in business all these years."

## The argument

Christianity mislabeled the natural as sinful, manufacturing guilt it then charges
to relieve. Strip the label and the seven deadly sins are simply appetites; envy
and greed are ambition, pride is self-respect, lust is the second-strongest
instinct after self-preservation. Man is an animal, "sometimes better, more often
worse" than the four-legged kind.

Two moves keep it from collapsing into hedonism. **Indulgence is not compulsion** —
compulsion is what unreleased desire becomes, so scheduled indulgence produces
control rather than dissipating it. And the ethic is reciprocal rather than
universal: "Do unto others as they do unto you," with altruism dismissed as
"sinning on the lay-away plan" and the [[Psychic Vampire]] identified as the person
who extracts obligation without ever asking for it.

## What it forbids

Worth recording precisely, since the reporting usually inverts it. The book
prohibits sacrificing animals or children, holding both "sacred" as the purest form
of carnal existence; forbids rape, child molestation, and bestiality; and permits
sexual activity of any kind only among consenting adults. Its chapter "On the
Choice of a Human Sacrifice" means destruction by curse — symbolic, at a distance,
of someone who has wronged you. The magician is told to supply ritual energy from
his own body, principally by orgasm, and the practice of killing for it is mocked
as cowardice: those who would "sooner chop the head off a goat" than "masturbate in
full view of the Jehovah whom they claim to deny."

The black mass, likewise, is called "a literary invention" of the Church, not a
Satanic rite — and a modern one would blaspheme not Christianity but "Eastern
mysticism, psychiatry, the psychedelic movement, ultra-liberalism."

## Sources

The book is substantially assembled from prior material — Ragnar Redbeard, Ayn
Rand, John Dee, William Mortensen — a fact this very edition documents in its
appendices. Treated separately: [[The Sources of the Satanic Bible]].

## Evidential status

> [!warning] Not an evidentiary work
> This is a manifesto, not an argument that offers itself for testing. Its
> empirical claims — that most illness is psychosomatic, that tear ducts are
> controllable like a bladder, that curses work best on skeptics — are asserted
> flat. The theory of magic is constructed so no outcome can disconfirm it:
> believers are receptive, and disbelievers are *more* vulnerable because denial
> "is the very ingredient that will create its success." See
> [[Unfalsifiable Inversion]].

## This edition

`raw/` holds an unofficial "Underground Edition" (v1.00, Hallowe'en 2006), a scan
bundled with four editorial introductions spanning 1969–2005 and a set of
appendices. The appendices are largely by **Temple of Set** authors — Michael
Aquino and George C. Smith — who broke with the [[Church of Satan]] in 1975. They
are partisan toward a rival organization and should be read as such, though their
textual comparisons are checkable and hold up.

The extras also carry LaVey prose in the first person plural that the Book of
Lucifer renders in the third — "as *we* feel that altruism is sinning on the
lay-away plan" against "because *they* feel that altruism is sinning on the
lay-away plan." Gilmore's introduction states the book's essays were expanded from
earlier Church monographs, so this is very likely one of those drafts printed
alongside the text it became *(inferred — the passage sits under no title in the
edition's contents)*. If so, the volume exhibits its own composition.

> [!conflict] Disputed
> LaVey's formula for the Church's public strategy is quoted in reversed forms
> within the same volume. Wolfe's 1969 introduction has "nine parts outrage to one
> part social respectability"; his 1976 revision has "one part outrage to nine
> parts social respectability"; Aquino's 1972 introduction has "nine parts social
> respectability to one part of the most blatant outrage." The later ratio is the
> one that matches the Church's actual conduct, but the source does not resolve it.
