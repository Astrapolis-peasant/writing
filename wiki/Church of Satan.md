# Church of Satan

The organization [[Anton LaVey]] founded in San Francisco on Walpurgisnacht 1966
to promulgate [[LaVeyan Satanism]]; [[The Satanic Bible (LaVey)]] is its canonical
text.

Two things made it a departure from earlier occult groups, per the source's own
introductions: it organized as a *church* rather than a coven, and it operated
openly rather than underground. Both were deliberate — the point was press
coverage, and it worked. Satanic weddings, baptisms, and funerals were staged with
reporters invited, and a photograph of a nude woman serving as altar ran on wire
services worldwide.

The organizing principle LaVey stated for this was a fixed ratio of outrage to
respectability, though the source quotes it inconsistently
([[The Satanic Bible (LaVey)]] records the conflict).

## Structure and succession

Local affiliates were called grottos. Authority passed from LaVey to Blanche Barton
on his death in 1997, and from Barton to Peter H. Gilmore in 2001, with Peggy
Nadramia as High Priestess from 2002. Gilmore wrote the 2005 introduction carried
by this edition.

Membership is described as deliberately restrictive — "no Satanic missionaries," an
"imperious few" — and the philosophy as "an integrated whole, not a smorgasbord."
Named members in the source include Jayne Mansfield, Sammy Davis Jr., and later
musicians King Diamond, Marilyn Manson, and Marc Almond.

## The 1975 schism

Michael Aquino, who wrote the 1972 introduction still printed in this edition,
subsequently left to found the **Temple of Set**. Several appendices in this
Underground Edition are Temple of Set documents arguing that the Church's
philosophy was derivative and that its "reactionary, anti-Christian" character was
a limitation their organization transcended
([[The Sources of the Satanic Bible]]).

The result is a volume containing both an admiring introduction by Aquino and later
material by him and his colleagues undercutting the book it introduces — an
artifact of the schism rather than an official Church of Satan publication.
