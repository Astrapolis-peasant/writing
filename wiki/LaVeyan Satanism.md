# LaVeyan Satanism

An atheistic, materialist philosophy that uses Satan as a symbol of carnal
self-interest and revolt, codified by [[Anton LaVey]] in
[[The Satanic Bible (LaVey)]] and organized as the [[Church of Satan]].

No deity is posited. "All religions of a spiritual nature are inventions of man" —
Satan names the part of himself man externalized and then learned to fear. The
proposed correction is to close that gap: if you insist on making a god in your
image, "why not create that god as yourself."

## What distinguishes it

Three commitments, each aimed at a different neighbor:

- **Against Christianity** — that it manufactures guilt over natural appetite and
  then charges to relieve it.
- **Against "white" witchcraft and the occult** — that altruistic magic is
  self-deceit, "sinning on the lay-away plan," and that practitioners who stand
  inside a protective pentagram while calling on the forces they claim to invoke
  are simply cowards.
- **Against Eastern mysticism** — that ego-dissolution is a doctrine for
  populations with nothing material to gain, and that its adherents are as
  compulsive as anyone else, merely better at claiming otherwise.

Note that this is one accusation aimed three ways, and it is not falsity but
insincerity. LaVey nowhere argues that Christianity is untrue — he argues that
Christians do not believe it, which is what the founding anecdote about the
burlesque and the revival tent is for ([[Anton LaVey]]). White magic is not
ineffective, its practitioners are lying about their motives. Buddhism is not
mistaken, Buddhists are merely better at concealing appetite. The charge is
Satanic Statement 3 — "undefiled wisdom, instead of hypocritical self-deceit" —
applied to every neighbour in turn.

The cost is that the book ends up with no argument against any of these positions,
only against the people holding them, and no sincere opponent can survive the
frame: adherents are self-deceived, and the successful are Satanists who have not
noticed. LaVey approaches this and declines to stop — "it would be an
over-simplification to say that every successful man and woman on earth is, without
knowing it, a practicing Satanist" — then argues it for the rest of the paragraph
([[Unfalsifiable Inversion]]).

## Why a religion rather than a philosophy

The book anticipates the obvious objection — if this is just doing what comes
naturally, why the trappings? The answer is the most substantive idea in it.
Intellectual acceptance and emotional acceptance are different faculties, and
psychiatry, "despite all the good it has done, has robbed man of wonder and
fantasy which religion, in the past, has provided." Satanism is positioned to fill
"the large grey void between religion and psychiatry": the ritual is
acknowledged fiction, deliberately entered. See [[Ritual as Psychodrama]].

This is what separates it from Objectivism or plain humanism, both of which supply
the ethics without the ceremony ([[The Sources of the Satanic Bible]]).

## The ethical core

Reciprocity, not universal benevolence. Love is finite and spending it
indiscriminately debases it; hatred repressed becomes illness, so it is given
ritual vent instead. Responsibility is owed to the responsible — hence the
[[Psychic Vampire]], the person who converts your conscientiousness into an
obligation you never agreed to.

On sex the position is permissive in a specific way: any activity, orientation, or
fetish among consenting adults, with the qualification that compulsory
permissiveness is its own bondage. LaVey is as contemptuous of the orgy-goer
proving his liberation as of the prude.
