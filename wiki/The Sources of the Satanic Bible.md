# The Sources of the Satanic Bible

[[The Satanic Bible (LaVey)]] is substantially assembled from four prior bodies of
work, and the edition in `raw/` documents this itself — partly in the Church of
Satan's own introduction, partly in appendices written by its schismatics.

| Section | Source | Nature of the debt |
|---|---|---|
| Book of Satan | Ragnar Redbeard, *Might Is Right* (1896) | Near-verbatim, reordered, with cuts and bracketed insertions |
| Nine Satanic Statements & Book of Lucifer | Ayn Rand, *Atlas Shrugged* (1957) | Argument and sequence, paraphrased |
| Book of Leviathan | John Dee's Enochian Keys (16th c.) | Reworked translation, "Satanized" wording |
| Law of the Trapezoid | William Mortensen, *The Command to Look* | Aesthetic theory repurposed as magical technique |

## What the comparison shows

Michael Aquino's appendix prints Redbeard beside LaVey line by line. The Book of
Satan is not influenced by *Might Is Right* — it is *Might Is Right*, lightly
edited. "In this arid wilderness of steel and stone, I raise up my voice" and the
inverted beatitudes of Book V are Redbeard's sentences.

The editing is itself informative. LaVey **cut** Redbeard's racial material: the
demand that "the racial mind" be "cleansed and drastically disinfected of its
depraved, alien" concepts, the line about "Jews and Christs and other degenerates,"
"no racial extermination," and "Cursed are the unfit, for they shall be righteously
exterminated." What remains is Redbeard's contempt for weakness with the
race-theory excised, and the bracketed insertions steer toward personal success —
"I break away from all conventions *[that do not lead to my earthly success and
happiness]*."

George C. Smith's appendix does the same for Rand, aligning each of the Nine
Satanic Statements against a passage from Galt's speech in sequence. His conclusion
is that Satanism is "Objectivism with an open mind concerning paranormal
phenomena," and that the Nine Statements served as the outline for the Book of
Lucifer essays.

## Concealment, then acknowledgement

The 1969 first edition carried no attribution. By 2005, Peter Gilmore's
introduction states it plainly: LaVey "prefaced these essays and rites with
reworked excerpts from Ragnar Redbeard's *Might is Right* and concluded it with
'Satanized' versions of John Dee's Enochian Keys," and lists Rand, Nietzsche, and
Mencken among the influences. The [[Church of Satan]] no longer disputes the
sourcing; the disagreement is over what it means.

> [!warning] Partisan appendices
> Aquino and Smith both left for the Temple of Set after the 1975 schism, and both
> essays argue that their organization supersedes LaVey's. Motive is plain. Their
> textual comparisons are nonetheless reproducible against the primary texts and do
> not depend on their conclusions.

## What survives the finding

Less than it first appears, and more than the schismatics allow.

The philosophy is genuinely derivative: nothing in the ethical content is original
to LaVey, and the Book of Satan is not authorship in any ordinary sense. But
"unoriginal" and "unimportant" are different charges. What LaVey contributed was
packaging — the decision to call it a *church*, to give a materialist ethic a
liturgy, a symbol, a calendar, and a name calculated for outrage. Rand's ideas had
been in print for twelve years without producing a single ritual chamber.

That contribution is exactly the one his own doctrine predicts.
[[Ritual as Psychodrama]] holds that intellectual assent is insufficient because
emotional
assent runs on other machinery — ceremony, image, the command to look. On that
theory, converting Objectivism into a religion is not a debasement of it but the
missing step. Smith's charge and LaVey's defense turn out to be the same
observation, read from opposite ends.
